How to write each section

Summary with no experience

Two sentences: who you are, and one proof. Try “Friendly, punctual student who handled cash at school fundraisers and is quick with numbers. Ready to learn your POS fast.”

Which skills to list

Use the words from the job ad: POS systems, cash handling, customer service, upselling, card payments, returns. List only what you can do on day one.

A first job

Count school, volunteer and family work. “Ran the bake-sale till for 3 weekends, balanced $900 in cash with no errors” shows the same skills.

12 cashier resume bullets to copy

  • Processed 180+ transactions per shift with 99.8% drawer accuracy.
  • Balanced a $1,500 opening and closing drawer daily with zero shortages in 12 months.
  • Served up to 40 customers an hour during peak periods while keeping lines under 5 minutes.
  • Resolved price and coupon issues on the spot, reducing manager calls by about 30%.
  • Signed up 25+ customers a month for the loyalty program.
  • Handled cash, card and mobile payments, and spotted 3 counterfeit bills.
  • Processed 20+ returns and exchanges per day following store policy.
  • Trained 4 new cashiers on the POS system and opening procedures.
  • Earned a 4.8/5 customer service score from shopper feedback cards.
  • Suggested add-ons at checkout that raised average basket by 8%.
  • Kept the checkout area clean and stocked, cutting restock requests by half.
  • Worked 30+ hours a week alongside full-time school, with a 100% on-time record.

Common mistakes

  • Vague duties. “Helped customers” says little. Say what you did and how many.
  • Missing numbers. Add transactions per shift, drawer accuracy, queue size or sign-ups.
  • More than 1 page. At this level, one page is enough. Cut older or unrelated jobs.
  • Fancy layouts. Columns and icons can break ATS software. Keep it one column.

Quick answers

One page or two?

One page. Cashier hiring managers skim, and you rarely have enough relevant content to need two.

Do I need an objective?

No. A short summary with your strongest proof is more useful than an objective statement.

How do I handle gaps?

Be brief and honest. List years rather than months, and add any caring, study or volunteer work from that time.
