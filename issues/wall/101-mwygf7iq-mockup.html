Are testimonials on your website legal? What small businesses must do

Updated 27 Nov 2026 · 6 min read · General information, not legal advice

Short answerYes. You can publish customer testimonials if they are genuine, you have permission to use the person's name, and you do not hide a payment, discount or relationship that would change how people read them.

What the FTC rule says (US)

The Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule has been in force since 21 October 2024. It bans fake reviews and paying for positive ones, and it expects honest disclosure.

Allowed

  • Thanking customers with a discount or gift for any honest review
  • Publishing a mix of reviews you received
  • Showing staff or family reviews with a clear disclosure

Not allowed

  • Rewards tied to a positive rating
  • Fake or AI-written testimonials
  • Hiding a relationship with the author
  • Cherry-picking so the page misleads

Permission to publish

Get a clear yes from each person before you show their name or photo. A recorded, dated consent beats a vague email thread.

UK: the ASA

The ASA expects testimonials to be genuine, from real customers, and used with their permission. You may need to keep proof.

Health, finance and legal services

These fields are restricted in many places. Check your own regulator's rules before publishing testimonials.

Sources

  • FTC: Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule, Questions and Answers (ftc.gov)
  • ASA: Testimonials and endorsements (asa.org.uk)
